# Kickoff message to Tonya

**Context:** Tonya is time-pressed and writes in short bursts. She flagged she was rushing a
deadline. This is built to be read on a phone in under thirty seconds and to ask for **one
thing** — the property and contact list. The ten detailed questions go to Matt, not her.

Also on the Notion page: <https://app.notion.com/p/41e5dc0bf9c380e08ef1ca9ac08f06a2>

## Teams / chat — the version to send

```
Hey Tonya - no rush on this, read it whenever.

I've got what you described: a link guys can tap or scan to send in a
maintenance request, so it lands in one place instead of your texts.
Maintenance only for now, like you said.

Two things would help whenever you get a minute:

1. A list of your properties and who you actually contact at each one -
   name of the hotel or apartments, the person, and whether they take
   requests by email, phone, or their own portal. That's the one piece
   only you and Jordan have, and nothing works without it.

2. Which hotel and which crew should we try first? I'd rather test it
   on one before turning it on everywhere.

I'll get the rest of the detail from Matt - what's the best way to
reach him?

One heads up so it's not a surprise: you'll probably see MORE requests
at first, not fewer. Guys report things they used to just live with.
The win is that each one shows up complete enough to forward without
calling anybody back - that's where your time comes back.

- Ernest
```

## Email version

```
Subject: Maintenance request link - one thing I need from you

Hi Tonya,

Following up on the maintenance requests - I've got a plan put
together and I don't need much from you to get moving.

The short version: a single link, which also prints as a QR code for
the hotels, where a guy reports a problem in about a minute. It lands
in one queue you and Jordan work once a day and forward to the right
property. Maintenance only to start, like you said.

What I need from you and Jordan:

- Your properties and maintenance contacts. For each hotel or
  apartment: the name, who you actually call or email, and whether
  they take requests by email, phone, or their own portal. Any format
  is fine - a napkin list is fine. This is the only piece nobody
  outside Plateau can give me, and the whole thing routes off it.

- Which hotel and crew to pilot. Two weeks on one property before
  anything goes wider.

I'll take the detailed requirements from Matt so this doesn't eat your
time - could you point me at him or loop him in?

One expectation worth setting now: request volume will likely go up
at first, not down. When reporting gets easy, people report things
they previously put up with. The time saving isn't fewer requests -
it's that each one arrives complete enough to forward without a
callback, and it stops arriving as an interruption.

Happy to walk through it whenever suits.

Ernest
```

## Why it's shaped this way

| Choice | Reason |
|---|---|
| Opens with "no rush" | She said she was rushing a deadline. Anything reading as another thing due today gets archived |
| Mirrors her own words back | "a link guys can tap or scan", "maintenance only for now" — she should recognise her request, not read a reinterpretation |
| Asks for one real thing | The property list. Everything is blocked on it, and it is the only piece that cannot come from Matt |
| Routes detail to Matt explicitly | Protects her time and gets the requirements, in one line |
| Sets the volume expectation now | If she first hears "more requests" from pilot data it reads as failure. Heard now, it reads as predicted |
| No link to the full page in the short version | A busy person given a document and a task does the document last. The ask has to survive alone |
| No questionnaire | The ten open questions are Matt's |

> **"A napkin list is fine"** is doing real work in the email version. The fastest way to not
> get the property list is to make it feel like a data-entry project — she will put it off
> until she has a spare hour, and she will not have a spare hour. Take it in any shape and
> tidy it up yourself.
