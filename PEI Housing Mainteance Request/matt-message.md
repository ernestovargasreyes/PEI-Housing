# Message to Matt

**Context:** Matt holds the detail from the conversation he had with Tonya. He is expecting a
real discussion, and he has context Ernest does not. So this is a **call request with a
pre-read** — not an ask, and not a questionnaire dressed up as an email.

Different shape from [`kickoff-message.md`](kickoff-message.md) on purpose: Tonya needed one
small concrete ask; Matt needs to be asked to correct the plan.

Tonya pointed me your way. She gave me the headline - a QR code or a

Two answers matter most, ideally by Tuesday, because I start building

1. Can worker names, cell numbers and room numbers go into an outside

   Microsoft 365? And could IT host a small web form in Azure that I'd

   all, no water, flooding, gas, smoke, no power, lockouts and
   break-ins all skip the form and go to that number.

Could we do 20-30 minutes Monday or Tuesday? If it's easier in
writing, here are the rest of the gaps:

SCALE
- How many properties, and roughly where across GA, FL, AL and TN?
- Rough split between hotels and apartments?

HOW IT WORKS TODAY
- Is there anything in place now - a spreadsheet, shared inbox,
  group text?

  and phone?

  and who escalates?

PEOPLE

  property, or whoever is free?

SCOPE
- Does anyone need to see what housing maintenance costs, or is that

- Is there a rule for how long we keep workers' contact details?

The one thing I've asked Tonya for directly is the list of properties
and who she contacts at each one. Nothing routes without it, so if
that list already exists somewhere, that's the most useful thing you
could send me.

Happy to send over what I've drafted beforehand if it helps.

Thanks,
Ernest

## If the call happens

**Live agenda page, open it during the call:**
<https://app.notion.com/p/41e5dc0bf9c381b0b2b2e7f33a5735f4>

Timeboxed, with the four question groups as checkboxes to tick as you cover them, answer
slots to type into live, and a **"what I got wrong"** section — which is the actual point of
the call.

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | What I understood from Tonya, said back. Let him correct it |
| 5–15 | The four question groups above. **Scale and "how it works today" matter most** — they decide pilot shape |
| 15–20 | Does the property + contact list exist anywhere already? |
| 20–25 | Pick the pilot property and crew, and who to tell |

**Leave with three things:** a property count, the pilot property, and either the contact
list or the name of whoever has it. Everything else is a bonus.

**Open by giving him room to disagree** — something like *"everything I've built came
secondhand through Tonya, so if any of this is off I'd rather hear it now than after I've
built it."* The most valuable outcome of this call is a wrong assumption caught early. Don't
spend the first five minutes selling the plan.

## Why it's shaped this way

| Choice | Reason |
|---|---|
| Leads with what's already drafted | He gave his time to Tonya once. Showing work first means he's correcting a plan, not repeating himself |
| "If your account and mine disagree, yours wins" | He was in the conversation. This invites a correction rather than a polite nod, and a wrong assumption surfaced now is cheap |
| Asks for a call, offers async | Ten questions by email gets a reply to three of them. The written list is the fallback, not the request |
| Groups the questions | Lets him answer the groups he knows and skip the rest without feeling like he failed a test |
| Repeats the property-list ask last | It's the real blocker. If it already exists somewhere, that single answer saves a week |
| Doesn't re-ask what Tonya answered | Nothing about QR vs link, scope, or why — she settled those. Re-asking signals you weren't listening |

> **Don't send Matt the full Notion page as the opener.** Offer it ("happy to send what I've
> drafted") and let him ask. A long document arriving unrequested from someone he hasn't met
> reads as homework, and the call is what you actually want.
